# Website Experience Audit Report Template

Adapt this structure to the website. Keep evidence near the claim it supports.

## Executive assessment

- Website and audit date
- Intended audience and purpose
- Overall assessment
- Three strongest qualities
- Three highest-priority problems
- Important coverage limits

## Scope and method

- URLs and routes inspected
- Viewports and input methods
- Representative journeys and success criteria
- Diagrams and examples sampled
- Authentication, data, or browser constraints
- Inventory size and sampling rationale
- Untested routes, diagram types, or interaction types

Use `Untested` when evidence is unavailable.

## Journey results

For each journey, record:

- goal and success criteria
- outcome: success, partial success, failure, blocked, or untested
- important actions and decisions
- friction, recovery, and confidence
- screenshot or other evidence

## Information architecture findings

Describe the apparent site model. Compare it with the user's likely mental model. Include naming, grouping, navigation, progression, cross-links, and dead ends.

## Diagram and explanation findings

For each sampled diagram, include:

- intended message
- actual interpretation
- entry point and reading order
- label, legend, density, and accessibility observations
- connection with surrounding prose
- concrete improvement

## Interactive-example findings

For each sampled example, include:

- learning or task purpose
- discoverability and instructions
- inputs, feedback, and resulting state
- reset and recovery
- keyboard, touch, responsive, and reduced-motion observations
- whether the interaction improved understanding

## Prioritized findings

Use this form for material findings:

### [Priority] Finding title

- **Surface:** Route, diagram, example, and viewport.
- **Reproduction:** Starting state and ordinary user actions.
- **Evidence:** What happened and where.
- **User effect:** What users cannot understand or do.
- **Reach:** Affected routes, examples, or audiences.
- **Recommendation:** A concrete change and intended outcome.
- **Acceptance check:** Observable proof that the change solved the problem.
- **Repeatability:** Repeatable, intermittent, or observed once.
- **Effort:** Small, medium, large, or unknown. Include only when supportable.

## Improvement roadmap

Group recommendations into:

- immediate blockers and high-risk problems
- quick improvements
- structural information-architecture or content changes
- diagram and interaction redesigns
- validation or user-research questions

## Strengths to preserve

Record effective patterns that future work should not damage.

## Evidence index and limitations

List screenshots, measurements, automated results, untested areas, and assumptions.

Use focused evidence images. State when a screenshot proves layout only and supporting text or measurements prove behavior.
